# How to write canon.md

Replace this file with your story's bible. The AI reads it before every piece of text it writes, so everything it needs to stay consistent with your world must be here. Write in second person, addressed to the player, in the same voice you want in the game. Keep it under 1500 words. Every character in this file must be plain ASCII or a CP437 graphic: straight quotes, plain hyphens, three dots instead of an ellipsis, no accents, no emoji.

## Start with the place and the date

Say where and when the story is set. The game itself is fixed in 1999, so keep to hardware and software that existed by then. Name your city or town. Give it weather, a smell and a light. The AI will repeat what you describe, so choose details you want to see many times: damp carpet, rain on glass, a fan that never stops.

## Say what the net is

Explain how the player experiences the network. Is it only phone lines, or is it also a shared dream? How does it feel when the carrier connects? What do rooms look like inside? If you name fictional hardware or companies, name them here once and plainly, so the AI uses the same names. Never use real brands or real people.

## Give the player a reason

State what the player wants and what was taken from them. A missing relative, a stolen board, a debt, a message left on a machine. This is the thread that every level pulls on. The player has one handle, and the AI is also told the player's real name and the name of the player's Shadow, so you may refer to them freely in your instructions to the AI, but keep this file in prose.

## Explain why the player cannot leave

The base game has no hang up. Every goodbye leads one level deeper. Say in your own terms why. A curse, a patch, a trap, a duty. Keep it as an image, not a rule: the receiver that plays a dial tone without end, the door that opens only downward.

## Describe the powers behind it

Name the company, the machine, the mind or the cult at the end of the story. Say what it wants. The cast file gives each recurring character a few lines, but the thing they all circle should be described here, so the AI can hint at it between your authored levels.

## Give the story a spine

Write the arc in a few sentences. What changes between the first act and the last? What is the final question? The default story follows four stages, but your pack may use fewer or more acts, as many as your anchors need.

## Name the rules of the world

If there is a single rule that explains the strange parts, put it in one sentence and repeat it. For example: a name only works if it is spoken aloud. Or: the clock always moves forward four minutes. A short rule gives the AI a limit it can build on.

## Things to leave out

Do not explain the psychology. Show it through images, voices and the choices the player makes. Do not list stats or mechanics. Do not use tables, bullet lists or code in this file; plain paragraphs and short headings work best. Never use curly braces here except the placeholders for the handle, the Shadow handle and the real name, which are written in braces as handle, shadow and real_name.

## A short worked example

You are in a town where the tide comes up through the storm drains every night. Your grandmother kept the only switchboard still in service, and she vanished with her headset on. The board in her cellar still rings. Whoever picks up hears the town's old voices, thousands of them, murmuring. You pick up. The line stays open. There is a door behind the switchboard that you do not remember, and when you try to put the receiver down, the floor tilts and you step through it.

Replace all of this text, including these headings, with your own canon. Then run the pack check to be sure it still validates.
