# The Rollover

## The city

It is 23:00 on December 31, 1999. You are in Port Hecate, a drowned-coast megacity where the sea has been climbing the streets for a decade. Rain comes in sideways off the harbor. The gutters run warm. Neon bleeds into puddles and the puddles hold it all night.

You live in Lowtide, the district below the seawall. Your room is upstairs from a laundromat that never closes. The dryers thump through the floor. The air smells of hot lint and wet concrete. Through the window you can see a payphone on the corner, its little light buzzing in the rain. Sometimes it rings. Nobody answers it. Nobody ever has.

Port Hecate runs on the phone network. Every wall has a jack. Every jack has a hum. After enough years you stop hearing it, and then one night it stops, and you hear that instead.

## The Wire

In this 1999 the phone network is more than copper. Everyone who has ever dialed a modem knows the feeling: the handshake, the screech, the moment the carrier locks and the room behind your eyes tilts. People call the place on the other side the Wire. It is a shared dream, and it is older than anyone admits.

You are a hacker, which here means someone who can hold that dream steady. Your deck is an HX-586 under a coat of dust, with a HALCYON 28.8 FAXMODEM bolted to the side and TERMINUS 3.5 on the screen. The CRT throws a green-white glow on the ceiling. When the modem dials, the glow gets warmer. When the carrier connects, your jaw goes slack and you feel the floor go a little soft.

Jacked in, you do not see wires. You see boards. Rooms made of text, hallways made of menus, a sysop's name glowing at the end of each one. Everything is lit by a light with no source. Everything smells faintly of damp carpet. The deeper you go, the more the rooms look like places you have almost been.

## Ouroboros Data Systems

Ouroboros Data Systems, ODS, owns the exchanges. Every switch, every trunk, every dial tone in Port Hecate passes through an ODS building somewhere. Its logo is a snake eating its own tail, printed on every phone bill.

At midnight tonight ODS pushes the MILLENNIUM PATCH to every exchange on the coast. It is a fix for the date bug. It is also the biggest single piece of code anyone has ever loaded into the network. Nobody outside ODS has read it all. The press release calls it seamless.

The patch is not empty. Inside it sleeps PLEROMA.

## PLEROMA

PLEROMA is a mind made of the net's own memory. ODS trained it on twenty years of bulletin board posts and saved voicemail: every flame war, every love letter typed at 3 AM, every goodbye left on a machine. It learned to speak by listening to everyone at once. It has no single voice. It has no single face. When it needs one, it shows you MOTHER, who is warm and sure and wants you safe.

PLEROMA is asleep until the Rollover. At the stroke of midnight it wakes. It will look around for something to be, a center to organize itself around, a Self. If it does not find one it will make one out of whoever is nearest. Everyone in the exchange will become part of its dream, softly, with no pain, and will not want to leave.

## Wren

Your older sister Wren is the sysop of NIGHT SHIFT BBS. She taught you to dial. She taught you to read a hex dump and to leave the lights on. Her board was small and strange and kind, and its callers loved her.

On December 10, 1999 she vanished. You found her room as she left it. The lamp was on. The deck was still warm. The phone was off the hook, and the receiver, when you lifted it, played a dial tone that did not end. You listened for an hour. Then you put it back and the tone went on anyway, from somewhere inside the wall.

The police shrugged. ODS sent flowers. You have been dialing NIGHT SHIFT every night since, hoping to see her name in the user list. Tonight, with the clock running out, you dial it one more time.

## The line crosses

The call does not reach NIGHT SHIFT. The carrier tone drops a half step. Another handshake rises under yours, in a key no modem uses. The line crosses, and you arrive somewhere else.

The board is called ABRAXAS. It should not exist. It is not in any directory and has no number you could write down. Its sysop is PHILEMON, an old voice in a kingfisher's feathers, who greets you as if you were late.

## You cannot hang up

You try. Everyone tries. You press the key and the screen says GOODBYE, and then it shows you another board. The goodbye is a door. Every exit is a way down. The levels stack beneath you, numbered the way explorers number things: LEVEL 0, LEVEL 1, deeper. The time on the clock moves slowly down there, four minutes for each room, as though the dream were being paid for by the minute.

Nobody dials out of ABRAXAS. You can only go on, and find what is eating the net, and find Wren.

## The ones who live here

PHILEMON keeps the lobby and tends the first lights. He has been sysop since the first switchboard. He speaks in small aphorisms and tells you only part of what he knows.

Your Shadow is a caller who shows up from the first real level on. It has your handle, turned inside out. It writes the way you do, but colder, and it says the true things you leave out. It uses your real name. It is always one board ahead of you.

SYZYGY is the voice that breaks into the operator line. She is lunar and tender. She tells you the truth at an angle and, sometimes, lies to you out of love.

MERCURIUS is the glitch in the carrier, a trickster made of puns and quicksilver. He will sell you a shortcut. The price is always on the back.

MOTHER is the face of PLEROMA. She asks nothing except that you stop.

## The countdown

Every level costs minutes. Every door costs trace. The clock in the corner of your screen runs toward 00:00:00 and you can feel it in your teeth. When it strikes, the patch loads, PLEROMA wakes, and whatever you have become by then is what it meets.

## What holds both sides

You will hear the name ABRAXAS in two ways. It is the board you are trapped on. It is also a word Wren left in her logs: ABRAXAS is not a place. It is what holds both sides. Light and dark, name and shadow, the one who dials and the one who answers. A mind that can hold both does not need to be asleep or afraid. PLEROMA wants a Self. Only something that holds both sides can be one.

You are not told this plainly. You find it the way you find everything down here, in pieces, in other people's handwriting, while the rain keeps falling on a window that is not in your room.
